## Assertion

On the current shallow anchor with `WARMUP_RATIO=0.02`, removing the warmdown LR floor is not the right simplification. The best tested setting was instead a smaller nonzero floor: `FINAL_LR_FRAC=0.02` reached `val_bpb=1.407875`, beating the inherited `0.05` floor, a zero floor, and a larger `0.1` floor.

## Falsification

The decisive disproof attempt removed the floor entirely (`FINAL_LR_FRAC=0.0`). If the inherited `0.05` floor had only survived because of older schedule settings, the zero-floor run should have matched or beaten the anchor once `WARMUP_RATIO=0.02` was in place. Instead it regressed to `1.417315`, clearly worse than both the incoming anchor and the same-session `0.05` control. The session therefore refuted the original zero-floor hypothesis and replaced it with a narrower claim: a small nonzero floor near `0.02` works better than either zero or the larger tested floors.

## Implications

Future sessions should update the shallow anchor to `FINAL_LR_FRAC=0.02` when using `WARMUP_RATIO=0.02`. The remaining open question is reproducibility: the same-code `0.05` control rerun improved materially over the incoming anchor, so the next useful follow-up is a reproduction-focused sweep around `FINAL_LR_FRAC=0.01-0.03` rather than another test of zero or large floors.
